# SpotScout

A public participation map where SEPTA bus riders click the stop they wait at and suggest a roof,
a bench, LED lighting, or a route information case. They can also say whether a shelter there should
carry public art, and what that art should say about the neighborhood.


1. **The specific participants/community I want to engage are** SEPTA bus riders who wait at stops with
   no shelter, bench, or lighting.
   - **Suburban corridor riders** Someone commuting on Route 104 along West Chester Pike who waits
     on a highway shoulder before sunrise or after dark in winter.
   - **Shift workers and late-night riders** A hospital or warehouse worker catching the
     60 at Allegheny Av & Hunting Park Av at 11 pm.
   - **People who wait longer or can't stand long** Older riders, parents with strollers, riders with
     disabilities, for whom a bench and a roof decide whether the bus stop is comfortable.
   - **Neighbors and local organizations** Block captains, business owners, congregations,
     community development corporations who know the block and its history.

2. **The contributions/input I am inviting them to make are** a specific bus stop (by clicking it on the
   map or entering the Stop ID from the sign) plus:
   - which upgrades they want there (roof, bench, LED lights, information case / route map; pick any);
   - how they use the stop (catch the bus here, live nearby, work nearby, would ride more if it had a
     shelter, think it's a good spot);
   - what times of day they're there;
   - whether the shelter should carry public art, and if so, what about the place has cultural
     significance for their community: people, history, languages, traditions, landmarks;
   - who should be contacted about the stop;
   - anything else about waiting there.

3. **The participants will benefit from contributing their input by** putting their stop on a shared,
   public record that's harder to ignore than a single complaint call. They can see that other riders
   flagged the same stop (counts and upgrade tallies per stop) or desire public art.

4. **The users that will use the collected data are**
   - transit and municipal planners who decide where shelters, benches, and lights go: SEPTA's bus stop
     planning staff, the City of Philadelphia's Office of Transportation and Infrastructure Systems (OTIS),
     which runs a bus shelter improvement program, and suburban township/borough staff and PennDOT along
     state roads like West Chester Pike
   - public art staff and artists like Creative Philadelphia / Percent for Art, Mural Arts looking for
     sites and community-sourced themes
   - community organizations and riders' advocates building a case for a specific corridor.

5. **The collected data will benefit those users by** showing where riders' demand is concentrated, what
   kind of upgrade is wanted at each stop, and when people are waiting (late-night demand points to
   lighting first). The outreach answers give planners a starting list of people to talk to before
   siting a shelter, and the art answers give artists neighborhood-sourced material.
